What I do

Turn complex material into simple tools for non-experts

Created a no-code tool for scientifically valid AI evals and red-teaming.

AI Safety Camp / AI evals tool

The tool enables domain experts with no coding or ML background to run real evals. This democratization can lead to a wider variety of safety issues in models being identified.

I did end-to-end user research, including personas, user tests and user interface design.

The tool isn't public yet, but is set to become so.

Test ideas fast and cheap - before they get expensive

Tested a prototype for an app for the Danish Police. Six user tests were enough to show the app wouldn't solve the underlying issue.

Danish National Police / Theft checker app

We prototyped and user-tested the concept in about a week. The user tests doubled as interviews, where rather than just looking at usability, I asked questions about their underlying reasoning (e.g. "why would you not contact the police?").

This taught us that people are unlikely to report sellers of suspicious second-hand items to the police, for a variety of reasons that have nothing to do with tech. So the real outcome was that the tests kept the client from building an app that wouldn't achieve their goals, which is worth more than the cost of one skipped prototype.

Boil complicated subject matter down to plain language

Wrote the copy for Giv Effektivt, an effective-giving platform. Based on the questions donors actually ask, not academic arguments.

Giv Effektivt / Website copy & design

The copy covered what you'd expect from a small NGO's site: why give effectively, how donations and the service work, who you can donate to, and the case for effective global health specifically.

The difference was in the writing: arguments for effective giving are usually explained with quite dense language. I cut the reading level and the length down drastically, and used a content-prioritization method on each page to decide on the core content: what do the readers want to know + what does the organization want them to do.

The platform hit all its goals in the first half year, including the strict member- and donor-count threshold Danish tax law requires for donations to become tax deductible.

I co-founded and chaired the NGO and led a team of volunteers in its development.

Prioritize based on evidence, not opinions

Product owner on Denmark's official platform for companies. Had ~1,000 people vote on the most-needed topics before designing anything.

Danish Business Authority / Virksomhedsguiden

The ~1,000-person vote was a top-task survey (people picked which topics they most needed help with). This was paired with around 40 interviews across different types of business owners and together those results decided which topics and pages got built first.

By identifying the pages likely to draw the most traffic and making those pages really good, we managed to create a useful platform on a strict deadline - without getting any complaints from the public, media, or politicians, which isn't always the case for public project of this size and attention.

Design robust systems people can easily use, even under pressure

Designed the IT systems behind Danish national elections. They've been in use for two elections, with no reported issues.

KOMBIT / Danish election system

My team built all the systems behind Danish elections, from scratch. Getting there was a structured, incremental process, one piece at a time: design, workshop with the project team and users, iterate, user test, iterate again, ship.

Validation was built into the cycle itself: nothing moved forward without being tested and workshopped with the people who'd actually use it. That meant I facilitated more than a hundred user tests and a dozen workshops.
